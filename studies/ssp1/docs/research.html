SSP1: a step after a smooth-terrain null.

Sample nine elevations along the local terrain-gradient normal at offsets −4 to +4 pixels. Project the observed profile and tanh step templates off the same smooth basis [1,t,t²]. The residualized template tests an odd-symmetric scarp, not simply a large slope. Two widths are fixed in advance: 0.7 and 1.4 pixels.

Q = I − X X⁺,      X = [1,t,t²]
r = Q z,           s = Q tanh(t / w)
amplitude = (r · s) / (s · s)
R²_step = (r · s)² / [(r · r)(s · s)]

This is a shape-fit feature, not a calibrated Bayes factor. Alluvial-fan terraces, paleoshorelines, roads and lithologic contacts can mimic it. Tangent persistence, gravity alignment and independently derived LiDAR/radiometric controls do not make those ambiguities disappear.

Label hygiene

The shared 35-channel cache is reused. The new 17-channel transform contains no fault-distance, coordinate or peer-prediction feature. Each whole hidden segment is removed from supervision. Models train outside the held quadrant plus a 15 px buffer, using only fold-visible labels and visible-only negative sampling. Visible test faults are masked pixel-exactly; withheld ones are not masked using the full catalogue.

Why 0.2778 is not evidence of a new physical mechanism

The Champion is exactly D2.8 outside a full-catalogue two-pixel collar; no dots were added. For the official metric, D = 0.2(TP + FP) + 0.8|G|. Removing redundant dots while keeping kernel true-positive credit fixed lowers the denominator. That is a plausible geometric benefit, not proof of what happened on the hidden truth. A removed dot covering a new nearby fault could instead hurt.

Only exact catalogue pixels are officially masked. A 200 m collar is a heuristic, not an organizer exclusion rule. File names such as “PINN” do not establish independent physics. The receipt and hidden truth needed for a causal explanation are not available.

Why there is no leaderboard point estimate

A withheld public segment is not guaranteed to be exchangeable with a newly mapped expert fault. Many peer files are identical or nested, so they are not independent calibration examples. Stored full-catalogue masks also contaminate naive hide-and-recover comparisons. A regression from those claims would suggest calibration that has not been demonstrated.

Different hidden fault sets can agree with everything observed publicly yet assign very different DTI to this candidate. The honest automatic local estimate is HOLDOUT-DTI with its stated CI and population. A precise hidden-score projection is not identified by this evidence.

Knowledge retained for future work
