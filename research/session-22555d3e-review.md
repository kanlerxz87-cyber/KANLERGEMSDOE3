These are historical, exact-byte geometry findings—not score measurements. That audit is not a complete current registry, and the nine page heads have since changed; nevertheless, the known historical references alone are sufficient to reject B2 as a unique submission under the literal >70% stop.

### What might explain the owner-reported value

A conservative, testable possibility is **emission efficiency**: removing weak or redundant dots near a known-catalogue buffer may reduce false-positive mass while retaining enough coverage of hidden faults. Under the competition's asymmetric DTI (`alpha=0.2`, `beta=0.8`) and triangular 300 m kernel, the direction is not guaranteed: discarded dots may have been near hidden faults and supplied recall/credit. A high owner-reported live value therefore cannot establish that catalogue thinning caused a gain, that the retained dots found new geology, or that the same method would score similarly on a spatially blocked holdout.

There is no controlled same-fold paired ablation for the add/remove step, no independently reproduced H33 B2 HOLDOUT-DTI with evaluator version / withheld count / 95% CI, and no organizer receipt. The precise causal contribution of geological targeting versus emission count, hidden-label proximity, fold choice, and evaluator version is unknown. **A new-file score estimate is not estimable.** The public leaderboard page and participant-side projections do not supply one.

## Pre-implementation hypothesis ranking

The four hypotheses already listed in [`research/hypotheses.md`](hypotheses.md) were ranked before implementation; this review does not assign any as a method lane. The ranking is qualitative expected DTI potential conditional on data and protocol access—not a numeric gain, score forecast, or evidence that a method is unique.

1. **Cross-field Morse–Smale junction persistence** — strongest conditional potential / medium cost. Combine signed, multi-scale RTP/TMI and gravity structure into persistent ridge/saddle junctions. Fault relays or intersections may disturb independent potential fields even where no single edge is strong. Distinct from pixelwise ridges and pointwise classifiers, but semantic novelty is not established: topology/gap-closure and phase-congruency are present in the peer ecosystem. Mimics include intrusive contacts, basin-fill boundaries, and survey levelling seams.
2. **Two-sided directional-fabric entropy break** — moderate conditional potential / medium cost. Compare opposing-side structure-tensor distributions across a candidate boundary using a divergence statistic; a buried fault may juxtapose differently oriented fabrics even without an amplitude scarp. The distinction is the two-sided distribution change, not another single-pixel orientation score. Mimics include angular unconformities, intrusions, cultural alignment, and survey blocks.
3. **Signed edge-migration stability under continuation** — low-to-moderate conditional potential / low-to-medium cost. Track signed zero-crossing trajectories across a fixed upward-continuation/scale ladder; persistent deep-source edges may survive as shallow responses fade. This differs from magnitude-only ridge persistence, but existing peer continuation/edge work makes novelty uncertain. Mimics include deep lithologic contacts, basin margins, and padding/boundary effects.
4. **Hydrochemical geothermometer residual corridors** — potentially interesting but **not rankable for this run** / high cost; placed fourth by present actionability, not by an asserted low geological potential. Condition chemistry/temperature residuals on lithology/elevation and test coherent corridors aligned with independent magnetic axes. Mimics include mixing, evaporation, groundwater advection, seasonality, pumping, and lab-batch effects.
