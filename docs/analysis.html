What the pixels actually say.

A technical reading of the registry stop, the reported B2 value and the limits of the local evidence.

01 / Uniqueness preflight

Why the run stopped

The pinned shared rule treats each finite value greater than zero as a dot. A registered dense surface is positive at every one of the 5,167,373 cells in the mirrored footprint. Every nonempty in-footprint candidate is therefore within three pixels of that surface at 100% of its dots, exceeding the >70% cutoff.

A second lattice witness covers 5,160,724 footprint cells within three pixels. With 37,654 candidate dots, at most 6,649 can be outside that neighborhood, so overlap is at least 82.34%. The dense witness blocks every nonempty budget; the lattice is an independent illustrative bound at that specific budget. These are local measurements under the literal rule, not an organizer decision or a complete registry scan.

02 / GEMSDOE32 H33–H33-2–B2

A plausible mechanism, not a receipt

The B2 file has 37,654 positive cells. Its support is exactly the 44,090-cell D2.8 reference after removing 6,436 dots within two 100 m pixels of the mirrored known-fault catalogue; no pixels were added. The reported 0.2778 is an owner/user claim without a linked organizer receipt. The public leaderboard does not identify TIFF filenames.

The organizer masks known faults pixel-exactly, so removing nearby dots could improve the index if they are false positives or redundant, while distant edges may capture unmapped structure. It could also remove valid nearby extensions or splays. Both explanations are scientifically plausible; neither the hidden labels nor the counterfactual score are available here.

For binary dots, with weighted true-positive credit T, dot count S, dot-side credit M, and truth count G, the official denominator is 0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8G. It is not generally 0.2S + 0.8G: truth-side and dot-side credit need not be equal. No improvement has been demonstrated using a common, leakage-controlled evaluator.

03 / Reproducibility caveats

Know what each check proves.

  • Public-mirror SHA-256 checks prove byte identity, not organizer-authenticated origin. The mirrored sample contains positive cells matching known faults, unlike the official total-absence example description.
  • The format inspection tests saved bytes against that mirror and the unchanged shared local gates. It is not a portal receipt, uniqueness decision or promotion.
  • The shared fold builder needs an upstream whole-segment, local-buffer correction; its scoring function alone cannot verify leakage-safe folds. The shared metric module also has a documentation error: the incremental truth credit can exceed one dot’s self-credit when several truth cells are covered. Its exact two-sided implementation is not shown to be wrong by that comment; the upstream wording needs correction. No private fork was made.
  • Evidence is a dated snapshot. A source HEAD change calls for re-audit; a live public rank does not establish which file produced it.
