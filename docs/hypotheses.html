Research shortlist

Four ways to test what is missing.

Ordered by qualitative potential information gain, not a numerical score forecast. These proposals have not been implemented, and an assigned method lane is still missing.

No geological experiment has run

A positive-everywhere registry witness prevents any nonempty candidate from passing the current literal uniqueness rule. No policy exemption is implied.

01 / Highest-priority proposal

Radiometric texture displacement

Use magnetic RTP/gradient axes as independent candidate lines. Compare paired K, Th and U texture windows on opposite sides for a coherent along-strike displacement; condition on detrended relief and estimate matching uncertainty.

Why missing faults? A fault under cover could offset geochemical patterns without a mapped surface trace. Alternative: a depositional fan, lithologic contact, airborne seam or unrelated alteration. The proposed paired-offset test differs conceptually from pointwise ratio fusion; novelty across all prior work is not established. Medium cost.

02 / Proposal

Cross-scale magnetic-pattern offset

Compare opposite-side RTP, TMI and gradient patterns at multiple smoothing scales. A consistent lateral offset could indicate concealed faulting; intrusive contacts, remanence or flightline artifacts can mimic it. Unlike a simple gradient maximum, this explicitly matches displaced textures. Medium cost; possible overlap with hypothesis 1.

03 / Proposal

Relief and lineament termination pairs

Seek repeated, coherent paired terminations in detrended elevation, slope and LiDAR scarp features across an independent geophysical axis. Drainage capture, erosion, fans and roads can mimic discontinuities. This proposes paired geometry rather than a standalone scarp score; missing LiDAR coverage must not be imputed as geology. Moderate-to-high cost.

04 / Proposal

Terrain-adjusted thermal asymmetry

Test shallow-probe temperature contrasts across independently proposed lineaments after accounting for terrain, geography and sampling season. Groundwater transport, seasonal effects and land use are alternatives. Sparse biased measurements make expected information lower and cost high; a thermal anomaly is not proof of a vent or resource.
