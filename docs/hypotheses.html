Cross-field Morse-Smale junction persistence

UntestedMedium cost

Core RTP/TMI (bands 2 and 14), TMI horizontal/vertical gradients (bands 3 and 9), isostatic gravity anomaly and gradients (bands 13, 18 and 11).

Physical signature
Build signed scale-space edge fields separately, extract Morse-Smale ridge/saddle graphs, and rank junctions that persist across magnetic and gravity fields without requiring high anomaly magnitude.

Two-sided directional-fabric entropy break

UntestedMedium cost

Core RTP/TMI and isostatic gravity (bands 2, 14 and 13), with detrended elevation and slope (bands 12 and 19) used only as terrain confounder controls.

Physical signature
Estimate multiscale structure-tensor orientation distributions on opposing sides of candidate axes and detect abrupt Jensen-Shannon divergence in directional fabric while anomaly amplitude remains moderate.
Why missing from the catalogue?
A fault buried by basin fill can juxtapose blocks with different structural grain even when no continuous scarp or high-gradient line survives at the surface.
Difference from prior work
Local code computes per-pixel coherence/orientation but does not compare two-sided orientation distributions or directional entropy across an axis. It is not another maximum-gradient, ridge-density or catalogue-distance feature.
Non-fault mimic
Angular unconformities, intrusive contacts, survey-block boundaries and aligned cultural noise.

03

Signed edge-migration stability under continuation

UntestedLow cost

Core TMI/RTP (bands 14 and 2), TMI gradients (bands 3 and 9), and depth-to-base surface (band 15).

Physical signature
Track signed zero-crossing locations through a preregistered Gaussian/upward-continuation scale ladder and score edges whose positions remain stable while shallow high-frequency responses decay.
Why missing from the catalogue?
A deep fault-related source contrast might preserve edge position under continuation beneath cover, whereas shallow mapped scarps and surface clutter may weaken or migrate with scale. The supplied depth-to-conductive-base band is not automatically geological basement depth.
Difference from prior work
Existing local work uses multiscale ridge magnitude and phase features but does not model signed edge trajectories or location migration as the feature. It must still be checked against newer peer Fourier-continuation work before any global-novelty claim.
Non-fault mimic
Deep lithologic contacts, regional basement margins and continuation artifacts near footprint boundaries.
Availability / viability
Uses locally obtainable core bands only. Boundary padding and physical continuation assumptions must be preregistered; experiment remains blocked by protocol prerequisites. Source ↗

Rank 3 qualitative information gain; cheapest falsifiable pilot, not a score projection.

H33 B2 / mechanism vs inference

Why thinning can help—and why that is not a forecast.

The official DTI credits a ground-truth pixel with its best nearby prediction, not the sum of every nearby dot. Redundant dots can add false-positive mass without improving coverage. Removing those dots can improve emission efficiency; removing the wrong dots loses recall. The pinned B2 mask is exactly the GEMSDOE30 mask after removing 6,436 dots within two pixels of the mirrored catalogue; none were added. This observed geometry does not isolate a new geological signal or prove why its reported result differed.

The triangular kernel has zero credit exactly at 300 m. At alpha 0.2 and beta 0.8, missed faults are penalized more heavily than false positives. It is therefore wrong to infer that sparser is always better, or that one fixed dot count should be copied across geological settings.

Staff clarify that existing catalogue pixels are excluded in both rounds. A fold-local catalogue mask must exclude only visible faults, not withheld positives. A final submission already zeroed with the complete catalogue cannot stand in for an honest hide-and-recover prediction.

What could mimic the signal?

USGS describe GeoDAWN radiometrics as surface geology/soil information and magnetics as subsurface structure/geology information. Variable terrain clearance, flight geometry and levelling can create persistent line-like signatures. Lithologic contacts may explain cross-layer edges without a fault; stream capture can explain apparent drainage offsets; groundwater transport and seasonal sampling can explain thermal residuals.

Even a correct mapped-fault holdout has population shift: mapped/exposed faults are not necessarily representative of concealed expert-labeled faults. AUC below the canary threshold does not prove that feature lineage is leak-free. A passing format validator says nothing about those scientific issues.
