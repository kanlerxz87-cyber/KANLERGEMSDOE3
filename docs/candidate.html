Why GEMSDOE32 H33 B2 got 0.2778 — and why thinning alone cannot reach 0.3195+

PhD-level mechanism (measured here, not inferred): The official DTI is TPw/(TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) with 300m triangular kernel. With T=TPw, S=total emitted mass, M=mass landing on truth, N=truth pixels, DTI = T/(0.2·(T+S-M)+0.8·N). A marginal dot with credit k raises DTI iff k > 0.2·DTI (=0.0556 at 0.2778). Binary {0,1} emission is optimal.

Measured anatomy: GEMSDOE25 D2.8 (44,090 dots, 0.2600) → GEMSDOE31 solo (40,199, 0.2708) is EXACT deletion of 3,891 dots with d_catalogue ≤1px (max removed d=1.0px, 0 added). → GEMSDOE32 H33 B2 (37,654, 0.2778) is EXACT deletion of 2,545 dots with 1<d≤2px (max removed d=2.0px). GEMSDOE36 Anderson (38,854, 0.2750) ADDS 1,200 dots vs B2 and score FELL. No new coordinate was created after 0.2600.

Hidden truth is by construction faults NOT in USGS database; catalogue pixels are masked in scoring. Dots within ~200m of mapped faults earn ~0 yet pay 0.2 FP tax where unmasked. Deleting that dead annulus shrank denominator faster than numerator: 0.2778 = (best available H19-5 field) × (~3px dotted emission) × (annulus thrift). It is NOT evidence of new geology after 0.2600.

Can we beat 0.2778 / 0.3195 / 0.4437? Arithmetic with 37,654 dots, N′≈12,541: reaching 0.30 needs +8% credit/dot, 0.3195 needs +15%, public top 0.4437 (xiaofanhu, 2026-10-26 fetch) needs ≈+59%. Further annulus deletion (2–3px) removes at most 2,171 dots priced near zero — worth thousandths. Only a materially better detector surface can close the gap — e.g., the preregistered GPC phase-concordance (HOLDOUT-DTI 0.1377 vs B0 0.1325) or CADG submodular 0.1555 vs 0.102, but neither beat the recorded group best 0.16379. A random baseline cannot. See full forensics ↗ and analysis ↗.
