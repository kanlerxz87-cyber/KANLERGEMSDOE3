Prior shortlist and confounders

  1. Dip-offset structural edges: compare basinward gravity/magnetic gradients and topographic breaks. Buried faults are plausible, but volcanic contacts can mimic them. High implementation and provenance cost.
  2. Radiometric contrast and survey seams: examine U/Th/K contrasts along geophysical edges. Lithologic contacts and variable soils can mimic alteration. Medium-to-high cost; official data bytes and usable coverage need verification.
  3. Visible fault tips and relay zones: derive structural context from fold-visible traces only. Erosional fan channels can mimic a relay. Medium cost; mapping bias can reduce transferability.
  4. Raw flight lines or deformation data: test independent line profiles or displacement only if official coverage, licensing and downloads are established. Pumping-related subsidence can mimic strain. High cost; not currently verified as viable.

These are conditional priorities, not predicted DTI improvements. Relevant sources: USGS GeoDAWN (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ), USGS structural discontinuities, and USGS blind geothermal play-fairway report.
