SCIENTIFIC ROADMAP

Better questions before
better predictions.

Four ranked, untested hypotheses. Each links a physical mechanism to an observable signature and an alternative explanation; none is a promised score.

The target is faults, not vents.

The organizer asks for faults that can be indicative of geothermal resources. Geothermal evidence may guide a detector, but a hot spring is not by itself proof of a fault, and an unmapped fault is not automatically a productive resource.

No new model was run. The named parallel lane was not supplied and the shared precomputed feature stack is absent. The following ideas are preregistered for later consideration; “new here” does not establish novelty across all sibling work.

Hypotheses, ranked qualitatively

01 · Higher potential / medium cost

Cross-flank magnetic-texture displacement

Layers: total magnetic intensity, reduced-to-pole and detrended elevation. Compare oriented textures on opposing flanks for coherent, cross-scale displacement corroborated by a terrain break. A concealed offset could reveal an unmapped fault. Volcanic contacts, fluvial magnetite or acquisition seams can mimic it. Distinct from simple edge-strength gates; exhaustive prior-art novelty is unverified.

02 · Moderate potential / medium cost

Lithology-aware magnetic–gravity discordance

Layers: magnetic and isostatic-gravity channels plus elevation and a separately verified geologic-map layer. Compare cross-scale edge orientation and polarity, controlling for mapped lithological contacts. A covered structural boundary could be missed by surface catalogues, but a non-fault rock contact can look similar. Coverage and licensing of the additional layer remain unverified.

03 · Moderate potential / medium cost

Age-consistent scarp progression

Layers: elevation/scarp features and an independently sourced surficial geology and age map. Seek adjacent surfaces whose scarp heights grow with age, not just a sharp slope. Repeated displacement may persist outside a catalogued trace. Fan incision, shorelines and incorrect age assignments are alternatives. Adequate age-map coverage remains unverified.

04 · Uncertain / medium–high cost

Sampling-corrected thermal asymmetry

Layers: INGENIOUS well/spring temperatures, sampling-effort information and cached geophysical channels. Test whether a local thermal contrast aligns with a geophysical boundary where a blind conduit is plausible. Depth, groundwater flow, climate and drilling density can create the same contrast. Related sibling methods already exist; a prior-art search and ablation are required.

Why the old leader is informative

Our independent raster audit shows that the claimed GEMSDOE32 H33-B2 file is the D2.8 dot mask after a two-pixel exclusion from the mirror catalogue. This is a nested emission family, not evidence of an independent detector. Fewer redundant dots may reduce weighted false positives while retaining useful kernel coverage, but only the hidden labels can establish the actual effect.

DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2 × FPw + 0.8 × FNw) Kernel weight = max(1 − distance / 300 m, 0)

See the organizer's definitions, including maximum predicted kernel credit per truth pixel and distance-weighted false positives. Do not replace this with an average of folds or a nearest-neighbor shortcut for arbitrary probabilities.
