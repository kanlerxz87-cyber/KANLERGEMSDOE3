01

Windowed potential-field spectral-slope break

UntestedMedium cost

Existing participant-mirror layers: RTP magnetic anomaly (`rtp`), total magnetic intensity (`tmi`), and isostatic gravity anomaly (`iso_grav_anom`); compare gravity and magnetic fields separately before any fused score.

Physical signature
Within preregistered overlapping windows, detrend and estimate radial log-power versus wavenumber slopes in fixed wavelength bands. Test for a co-located, abrupt change in the long- versus short-wavelength decay regime across a potential-field boundary; do not use catalogue distance or known-fault locations as features.
Why missing from the catalogue?
A buried basement fault may juxtapose source-depth or magnetic/density domains beneath alluvium. A change in spectral decay can persist when a crisp surface scarp or single-scale edge is absent.
Difference from prior work
The repository and current GEMSDOE32 source already contain related Fourier upward-continuation, local-wavenumber, Euler/source-depth and multiscale edge families. The focused local/source search found no exact implementation of a windowed radial spectral-slope change point. This is a narrow distinction, not a claim of global novelty; the current peer scan is not exhaustive of all repositories or papers.
Non-fault mimic
Intrusive or lithologic contacts, basin-margin source mixing, survey-line levelling seams, window-boundary leakage and regional field trends.
Availability / viability
Planning hypothesis only, not executable now. The three named bands are in the local prepared inputs, but those inputs are participant-mirror bytes and not organizer-authenticated. A centrally reviewed feature/channel and digest would be needed; the canonical 35-channel cache is absent, no lane is assigned, and the current evaluator/registry gates fail. Do not compute or rebuild a private cache. Source ↗
Expected DTI improvement
Qualitative only: plausible small-to-moderate positive HOLDOUT-DTI increment if the buried-source contrast is repeatable; equally plausible zero or negative increment. No numeric DTI or leaderboard projection is defensible.
02

Fault-offset geometry of non-fault geologic contacts

UntestedHigh cost

New official bedrock/geologic-map unit polygons and their non-fault contact boundaries, with existing RTP and gravity used only as independent corroboration. Exclude mapped fault features from predictors and record the exact map source/scale.

Physical signature
Detect repeated, geometrically compatible truncation or offset of multiple lithologic contacts across a narrow corridor, then test whether that corridor aligns with an independent potential-field edge. Never ingest the map's fault linework as a feature or use hidden/withheld catalogue labels.
Why missing from the catalogue?
A concealed structure can displace or terminate stratigraphic contacts even where the fault itself is unmapped; repeated offsets across distinct units are more diagnostic than a single contact edge.
Difference from prior work
The current prepared stack has no non-fault geologic-contact geometry layer. Local and peer methods use several fault, potential-field, geochemical and terrain proxies, but this proposed predictor is the mapped displacement of non-fault unit contacts—not a fault-map overlay. No verified contact dataset has been assembled here.
Non-fault mimic
Unconformities, erosional truncation, facies changes, map-sheet joins, inconsistent map scale and georeferencing error.
Availability / viability
NOT CURRENTLY VIABLE. The USGS National Geologic Map Database is an official discovery index, but exact map coverage, scale, license/metadata, contact geometry and obtainable bytes for this footprint were not verified in this sandbox. Verify these before proposing an experiment; do not treat the index itself as an available feature layer. Source ↗
Expected DTI improvement
Unknown and coverage-dependent; at best a conditional positive HOLDOUT-DTI increment where suitable maps resolve repeated offsets. No numeric projection. If official coverage is too coarse or absent, expected gain is zero and the hypothesis is dropped.
03

Hydrothermal-alteration contrast crossing independent structure

UntestedHigh cost

Potentially new NASA/USGS LP DAAC ASTER surface-reflectance observations, with existing RTP/gravity edges as independent structural context. Product version, acquisition dates, cloud/vegetation masks and local coverage must be pinned before feature design.

Physical signature
Test whether a spatially coherent mineral/alteration proxy changes across or terminates against a potential-field lineament, rather than scoring the alteration anomaly alone. Use only label-free spectral ratios with documented atmospheric, surface and masking treatment.
Why missing from the catalogue?
Faults can provide permeability for hydrothermal fluids; an alteration halo or its structural termination may reveal a concealed pathway without a mapped surface trace.
Difference from prior work
No ASTER reflectance/alteration raster is among the 19 prepared bands, and the local/current peer search did not find this exact independent satellite alteration layer. Existing geothermal, temperature and geochemistry ideas overlap in purpose, so only the sensor/source is distinct; global method novelty is not established.
Non-fault mimic
Clay-rich sediment, lithologic mineralogy, playa/saline soils, vegetation, albedo and acquisition/atmospheric artifacts; alteration may also be absent along a real fault.
Availability / viability
NOT CURRENTLY VIABLE. NASA/USGS LP DAAC is an official data portal, but the exact product, access requirements, acquisition coverage, usable pixels and downloadable bytes for the AOI were not checked or obtained. Verify all of those first; do not infer availability from the portal landing page. Source ↗
Expected DTI improvement
Highly uncertain and indirect; likely zero outside exposed, sparsely vegetated altered ground. No numeric HOLDOUT-DTI or leaderboard estimate. It should rank below the core-data test unless official data coverage and an independent held-out signature are demonstrated.

H33 B2 / mechanism vs inference

Why thinning can help—and why that is not a forecast.

The official DTI credits a ground-truth pixel with its best nearby prediction, not the sum of every nearby dot. Redundant dots can add false-positive mass without improving coverage. Removing those dots can improve emission efficiency; removing the wrong dots loses recall. The pinned B2 mask is exactly the GEMSDOE30 mask after removing 6,436 dots within two pixels of the mirrored catalogue; none were added. This observed geometry does not isolate a new geological signal or prove why its reported result differed.

The triangular kernel has zero credit exactly at 300 m. At alpha 0.2 and beta 0.8, missed faults are penalized more heavily than false positives. It is therefore wrong to infer that sparser is always better, or that one fixed dot count should be copied across geological settings.

Staff clarify that existing catalogue pixels are excluded in both rounds. A fold-local catalogue mask must exclude only visible faults, not withheld positives. A final submission already zeroed with the complete catalogue cannot stand in for an honest hide-and-recover prediction.

What could mimic the signal?

USGS describe GeoDAWN radiometrics as surface geology/soil information and magnetics as subsurface structure/geology information. Variable terrain clearance, flight geometry and levelling can create persistent line-like signatures. Lithologic contacts may explain cross-layer edges without a fault; stream capture can explain apparent drainage offsets; groundwater transport and seasonal sampling can explain thermal residuals.

Even a correct mapped-fault holdout has population shift: mapped/exposed faults are not necessarily representative of concealed expert-labeled faults. AUC below the canary threshold does not prove that feature lineage is leak-free. A passing format validator says nothing about those scientific issues.
